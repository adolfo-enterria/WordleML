What it has learned

How the blank AI plays (game 0)

It knows the rules: after each guess it keeps track of which of the 2,315 answers still match the colors, and for any word it can work out how that guess would split them. Once only one word is left it says it.

What it doesn't know is how costly a situation is: how many more guesses it will need with, say, 20 words still possible. A blank AI thinks every situation costs the same, so every guess looks equally good and it plays random valid words: about 6 guesses per game, some games dozens.

It learns that curve from its own games. Then, for each of the 12,972 valid words, it adds up how costly the leftover groups would be (winning right away costs nothing) and plays the cheapest. Nobody tells it when to test letters with a word that can't win, or when to go for the win: that comes out of the sum.

It knows the rules and only guesses words that could still be the answer, picking at random among them. Each guess's colors rule out most of the 2,315 words, so even a blank AI finds the word in a handful of guesses (about 4.1 on the official list: typically ~130 words left after one guess, ~9 after two, ~2 after three).

In this mode it can never play a probe word (a word that can't win but tests several letters), which is why it can't reach the best scores no matter how long it trains.
